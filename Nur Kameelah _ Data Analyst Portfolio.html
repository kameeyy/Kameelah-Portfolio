Predictive Maintenance for Centrifugal Pump Bearings

University project · Deep learning · Explainable AI · Dashboard

Problem

Unexpected bearing failure means costly downtime and safety risk, and fixed-interval maintenance can act too early or too late. The aim was a continuous Health Index, from 1.0 (healthy) to 0.0 (failure), that shows degradation as it happens.

What I built

  • A pipeline on the public XJTU-SY run-to-failure bearing dataset (15 bearings, 3 operating conditions)
  • 34 statistical and spectral vibration features, scaled using training bearings only to avoid data leakage
  • Leave-one-bearing-out testing, noise augmentation and 20-step sliding windows
  • Three models compared: Random Forest, BiLSTM with multi-head attention, and CNN-BiLSTM
  • SHAP explanations of the features driving each prediction
  • A four-page Streamlit dashboard with Health Index monitoring and warning and critical alerts

Result

On the best condition (35 Hz, 12 kN), BiLSTM with attention reached R² 0.975 and RMSE 0.036. Across the two valid conditions its mean R² was 0.746, ahead of Random Forest (0.661) and CNN-BiLSTM (0.427). On the 37.5 Hz condition its RMSE of 0.188 beat the 0.253 reported by a published attention-GRU method on the same test bearing.

What the results show

SHAP ranked horizontal mean absolute value, energy and log energy as the top features. That fits the physics: vibration energy rises as a bearing wears.

Limits, stated honestly

  • In the 40 Hz condition every model failed, because the test bearing lasted 22 times shorter than the training bearings. That is a dataset limit.
  • On the 37.5 Hz condition, Random Forest scored a higher R² than the BiLSTM.

Next steps

Connect to live sensor streams, use transfer learning for the lifespan mismatch, validate on real industrial pump data, and add sensors such as temperature and motor current.

KPI Tracking System for a Maintenance Department

Internship project · Power Apps · SharePoint · Power BI · Agile

Register KPI→Superior approval→Monthly update→Power BI dashboard

Problem

More than 300 employees kept their KPIs in separate Excel files. Every month, designated staff collected and merged them before uploading to Power BI, which took time and risked errors.

What I built

  • A Power Apps app with 5 screens: Home, Register KPI, Monthly Update, Approval and Guideline
  • Two ways to submit: one by one, or bulk paste from an Excel template
  • Dropdowns and Power Fx validation rules to keep entries consistent
  • An approval flow so superiors review KPIs before they count
  • SharePoint lists as the database, with delegation-friendly queries to work past the 2,000-row limit
  • Power BI dashboards for submission status, update compliance and KPI summaries, filtered by section and superior

Result

One structured system replaced manual Excel collection. I deployed it to the whole department with training, user guides and a helpdesk, and users can export their own KPIs to Excel.

How I worked

I followed an agile approach over a 20-week plan. User testing shaped the product: feedback led to filters and search in the approval view, sorting, and an update-compliance page in Power BI.

Challenges and fixes

  • Slow with large data: delegation-friendly queries and SharePoint indexing
  • Wrong values in Power BI: restructured the dataset and added validation
  • Slow refresh: optimised queries and used incremental refresh, though scheduled refresh stayed slow
  • Users new to the tool: guides and ongoing support

What I'd do next

Move the data from SharePoint to Dataverse for better performance, and add caching to speed up responses.

Data model (Power BI)

  • A many-to-one relationship between the KPI table and the employee table, linked by email
  • DAX calculated columns for submission status (Not Started, In Progress, Approved), IsUpdated, PercentageUpdated and MonthlyUpdated

Handover

I wrote an admin guide for the next owner: editing in-app guidelines, publishing Power BI updates, which parts of the approval page need care, and how the relationships and DAX columns work.

What I built

  • SharePoint as the data store, with Power Automate for approvals and notifications
  • A Power BI report linked from the app for status monitoring

Result

Issues could be reported, approved and tracked in one system, and used for maintenance and troubleshooting.
